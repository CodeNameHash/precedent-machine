## Deliverable (honest conversion semantics)

Schedule 13E-3 filing deadline in days from signing; (b) the
meeting-scheduling deadline in days from mailing or SEC clearance; (c)
adjournment/postponement caps — maximum number of adjournments and maximum
days (per-occasion or aggregate); (d) the board-recommendation inclusion

a quoted PRESENT claim. Record-date mechanics, adjournment REASON taxonomy,
mailing deadlines, and everything recommendation-CHANGE-shaped stay out
(sections below give each its ruling).

## 2. Value parser: `proxy-meeting-count-parse.js` (the ONLY parser in this
slice — one pure module, `measurement-date-parse.js` contract shape: typed
`{outcome:'RESOLVED', canonical_value, matched_text}` /
`{outcome:'ABSTAIN', reason}`; never a throw on prose, never arithmetic,
never repair; `PROXY_MEETING_PARSE_VERSION` threaded into the resolution
receipt, P1 M-6). Two exported functions.

**`parseDayCount(quote)`** — grammar = the TERMR `cure-period-parse.js`
grammar, extended by exactly two frozen devices (the financing
one-element-allowlist convention: frozen sets, never skip-word heuristics).
**Build-order dependency, named (unbuilt as of this writing — `lib/
canonical-v2/native-producer/` contains no `cure-period-parse.js`; the
TERMR slice itself is unbuilt):** per the same first-merger-builds
convention as the producer-prompt-registry seam (section 3), whichever of
this family's slice or the TERMR slice merges first BUILDS
`cure-period-parse.js`; the later slice adds only its extension. If this
family merges first, the module must be built standalone from the base
grammar restated here so section 2 and test 1 are implementable without
waiting on TERMR: candidate tokenization is digit runs (optionally
parenthesized, optionally preceded by a spelled-numeral word) immediately
adjacent (across whitespace only, no intervening punctuation beyond the
parenthesization itself) to a day word from the frozen set (`day` /
`days` / `Day` / `Days` / `Business Day` / `Business Days`, extended below
by the calendar forms); the adjacency rule requires the day word to
immediately follow the digit run (plus optional parenthesization) with no
intervening tokens; hybrid parenthesized-digit-after-spelled-numeral
handling and all ABSTAIN reasons are as listed below, which are this
slice's devices whether or not the TERMR module already exists in the
tree at merge time.

- Day-word set grows by the calendar forms: `calendar day` / `calendar
  days` / `Calendar Day` / `Calendar Days` (two-word forms, exactly as the
  Business-Day forms already are). Grounded necessity: "thirty (30)
  calendar days" (`ea987120`), "aggregate of 15 calendar days"
  (`db48f54c`), "ten (10) calendar days" (`15f81713`) — under the
  unextended TERMR adjacency rule every calendar-day cap ABSTAINs
  `NO_DAY_COUNT`, and calendar-day drafting is one of this family's two
  modal unit forms (pack variant warning 1).
- ORDINAL REFUSAL: a candidate digit run carrying an immediately attached
  ordinal suffix (`st|nd|rd|th`, as in "the fortieth (40th) day"
  `ea987120`, "the first (1st) business day" `15f81713`) → typed ABSTAIN
  `ORDINAL_DAY_FORM` → review, never resolved. Converting "not later than
  the fortieth (40th) day following the first mailing" into a 40-day
  deadline is legally sound but it is INTERPRETATION (the TERMF
  `ANNIVERSARY_PHRASE` ruling, verbatim): a mechanical parser that
  interprets once will be extended to interpret twice; Ben confirms these
  in review at trivial cost.
- Hybrid "thirty (30) days": the parenthesized DIGIT is the candidate; a
  preceding spelled numeral from the frozen word→value table that
  CONTRADICTS the digit → ABSTAIN `SPELLED_DIGIT_MISMATCH` → review.
  Spelled-only counts → ABSTAIN `NON_LITERAL_NUMERAL` → review, never a
  lookup-and-resolve (the table detects contradiction; it never reads
  numbers).
- Exclusions before candidate counting (P1 tokenizer discipline, inherited
  verbatim): section references (this keeps Red Hat's "Section
  5.01(c)(iii)" cross-reference keys out of the candidate set), calendar
  dates, clock times, currency literals (this is the whole money fence —
  the Dyax "$77,000,000" co-located bytes are excluded before counting,
  and NO currency path exists in this module; boundary item 6, asserted
  structurally by test 1).
- Hour-denominated periods ("no later than twenty-four (24) hours", pack
  value inventory — SEC-comment notification prose): "hours" is not a day
  word, so hour counts are never candidates; a quote whose only period is
  hour-denominated ABSTAINs `NO_DAY_COUNT` → review. No unit conversion,
  ever (the no-shop hour-period ruling, adopted verbatim: silent
  hours→days conversion is the reads-as-correct corruption).
- Two or more surviving day counts → ABSTAIN `MULTIPLE_DAY_COUNTS` →
  review. This is the DESIGNED outcome for the family's dominant compound
  drafting: Red Hat's triple-cap paragraph (30 days / 10 Business Days /
  10 days), Envestnet's "respectively" pair ("no more than thirty (30)
  calendar days and ten (10) Business Days, respectively"), Dyax's
  count + aggregate sentence. The parser NEVER picks and NEVER
  distributes a "respectively" pairing — the pairing map is legal
  content, and the producer is instructed NOT to split
  respectively-constructions at all (section 3): they queue whole, for
  Ben. Zero candidates → ABSTAIN `NO_DAY_COUNT`.
- `day_kind`, `limit_basis`, `anchor_kind` are NOT decided here — the
  parser returns `matched_text` with the governing clause window; the
  resolver corroborates the labels against it (section 4).
- Canonical form: strict digits (`^\d+$` after extraction), round-trips
  `NON_NEGATIVE_DECIMAL_STRING`.

**`parseAdjournmentCount(quote)`** — occurrence counts, not day counts:

- RESOLVES: a literal digit (or parenthesized digit in the hybrid form)
  whose governing next words match a FROZEN occurrence-governor set:
  `time` / `times` ("a total of three (3) times", `9033721b`) or the
  two-word lookaheads `such postponement` / `such adjournment` /
  `such adjournments` ("no more than two (2) such postponement or
  adjournments", `db48f54c`). Any other continuation disqualifies the
  candidate (frozen set, not a heuristic).
- Day-word continuations are structurally excluded here (they are
  `parseDayCount`'s candidates); a quote carrying both a count cap and a
  duration cap is TWO claims — the producer splits (section 3), each

- Spelled-only occurrence caps ("more than two times", `4b604d7a`) →

- Multiplicity → `MULTIPLE_OCCURRENCE_COUNTS`; zero → `NO_OCCURRENCE_COUNT`.
  Same hybrid/contradiction rules as parseDayCount.

The parser never computes: "not postponed or adjourned more than an
aggregate of 15 calendar days" resolves as the count `'15'` with the
more-than/aggregate structure preserved in the quoted evidence —

was (or was required to be) originally scheduled", `5f710c6c`) and consent
