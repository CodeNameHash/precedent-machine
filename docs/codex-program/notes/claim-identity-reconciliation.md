## 1. Verifying the two defects before designing anything

Read `resolution.json` and `validation.json` from the committed evidence run directly (not `adapter-result.json` or `run-receipt.json`, both too large, per the brief).

**Defect 1, confirmed by direct read.** `resolution.json`'s `resolved` array has 9 entries. Three of them are `TERMINATION_FEE_TRIGGER` claims, all `concept_key: TERMF-TARGET`, all `canonical_value: SUPERIOR_PROPOSAL_TERMINATION`, from `section_reference` 7.1, 7.3 and 7.1(c)(i) respectively, each with its own distinct `subject_occurrence_id`. Two more are `TERMINATION_FEE_TAIL_PERIOD_MONTHS` claims, both `canonical_value: "12"`, from 7.3 and 7.3(b)(iii). Confirmed the named non-duplicate is real and distinct: two `TERMINATION_FEE_AMOUNT` claims both valued `15000000.00`, one `concept_key: TERMF-TARGET` (the Company's second limb) and one `TERMF-REVERSE` (Parent's fee) -- different `concept_key`, different `party`, genuinely different facts.

**Defect 2, confirmed by direct read.** `validation.json`'s `residuals` has one entry, `reason_code: SEMANTIC_REFERENCE_UNRESOLVED`, on a `TRIGGERED_BY` relationship whose `source_occurrence_id` (`610595e1...`) matches the 7.3 citing claim's own `subject_occurrence_id`, but whose `target_occurrence_ids` (`421dbeb9...`) matches no `subject_occurrence_id` anywhere in the published claims. `validation.json`'s `quarantines` has one entry for the same relationship, `reason_code: UNRESOLVED_RESIDUAL`. The run is `accepted: true` only because the validator correctly refuses to publish a reference to nothing -- the defect is that the relationship was minted at all.

## 2. Tracing both defects to their mechanism

Read `lib/canonical-v2/source-structure.js`'s `buildProvisionInstance`: `provision_instance_id` is `contentId('PROVISION_INSTANCE/V1', {source_occurrence_id, canonical_text_id, document_hash, absolute_start, absolute_end, concept_key, party, ordinal})`. Read `candidate-resolution.js`'s `finalizeTerminationFeeClaim`: it mints (or reuses, via a `provisionsByGroupKey` map) one provision per `groupKeyFor({section_reference, concept_key, party})`, and every claim's `subject_occurrence_id` is that provision's `provision_instance_id`.

**Root cause of defect 1.** Provision identity is scoped to "which section the candidate happened to be dispatched under" (`absolute_start`/`absolute_end` of the WHOLE dispatched section, plus a monotonically-increasing `ordinal`), not to the underlying legal fact. Citation-following causes the identical ground to be read three ways -- once as part of the whole Section 7.1 (pass A), once as a bare cross-reference in Section 7.3 (pass A, borrowing 7.1(c)(i)'s corroborated code), and once as the independently-dispatched sub-clause 7.1(c)(i) itself (pass B) -- and each dispatch mints its own provision because `groupKeyFor` keys on `section_reference`, which differs all three times.

**Root cause of defect 2.** Read `mintCitationTriggeredByRelationship` (as it existed before this fix): it minted its OWN guess at the cited claim's provision, via a fresh `mintProvision({section: citedSection, conceptKey, party, ordinal: 1, ...})` -- `ordinal` hardcoded to `1`. The REAL provision for that same `(section_reference, concept_key, party)` triple, minted later by `finalizeTerminationFeeClaim` when the cited candidate's own entry is processed, gets whatever ordinal `provisionsByGroupKey.size + 1` happens to be at that point in the run's own processing order -- 6, in the real Modiv run, not 1. Confirmed this precisely: replayed the real recorded `run-receipt.json` through the unmodified resolver and printed the relationship's `target_occurrence_id` alongside every published claim's `subject_occurrence_id` -- the guess (`8f285e34...`) matches nothing published; the citing side's own `source_occurrence_id`, by contrast, IS published (only the cited side was ever guessed).

Two contributing facts, verified directly: (a) `mintCitationTriggeredByRelationship` runs INSIDE `handleFeeTriggerCandidate`, at the moment the CITING candidate resolves -- but the cited section is typically a pass-B follow-up dispatch, processed LATER in `runReceipt.compiled_candidates`, so its real provision does not exist yet; (b) the function never even checked `provisionsByGroupKey` for an existing entry -- it unconditionally re-derived a fresh guess.

**Shared root cause, as the brief anticipated.** Both defects trace to the same property: this resolver's occurrence identity is a function of *when and under what section a candidate happens to be processed*, not of the fact itself. Defect 1 is that property producing multiple identities for one fact. Defect 2 is a second piece of code trying to *predict* an identity that property makes unpredictable in advance.

## 3. Design for defect 1: chosen approach and why

Three options were on the table per the brief: a narrower identity, an explicit merge step, or a precedence rule.

**Rejected: narrower identity at the `buildProvisionInstance` level.** `buildProvisionInstance` is shared by every concept family in this 9000-line resolver, not just termination fees. Removing `absolute_start`/`absolute_end`/`ordinal` from its hash payload would change every `provision_instance_id` in the entire system, for every existing run, breaking the "three-call path stays byte-identical" requirement outright and reaching far outside this task's scope. Also insufficient on its own: even a narrower identity keyed to each claim's OWN evidence span would not converge Modiv's three sightings, because the model quoted slightly different byte ranges for the "same" clause across the three dispatches (a 7.1-whole read captures a longer span than the 7.1(c)(i)-only read of the identical clause). Convergence requires comparing sightings against each other, which a pure hash function over one sighting's own inputs cannot do.

**Chosen: an explicit reconciliation pass, with a precedence rule for picking the survivor.** Implemented as `reconcileDuplicateTerminationFeeSightings`, called once, after `resolveCandidates`'s main candidate loop AND after the (optional) v1v2/lexical-disagreement wiring steps that can themselves reassign the `resolved`/`reviewQueue` bindings -- so it always operates on the final set.

Detection: `factKeyForResolvedEntry(entry)` returns `${concept_key}::${party.value}::${claim_definition_key}::${canonical_value}` for `TERMINATION_FEE_TRIGGER`/`TERMINATION_FEE_TAIL_PERIOD_MONTHS` entries, `null` for everything else (in particular `TERMINATION_FEE_AMOUNT`, discussed below). Two resolved entries sharing a fact key are sightings of the same fact; this is the identity a claim SHOULD have, per the brief's own framing ("one Superior Proposal termination ground carrying one fee consequence") -- the seven `FEE_TRIGGER_CODES` are a closed, governed enum of legal categories, not per-clause identifiers, and this codebase's own established pattern (`ordinal`-based coexistence of the Company's two DIFFERENT fee limbs under one shared provision, see `docs/codex-program/notes/per-limb-fee-amount.md`) already treats `canonical_value` as the thing that distinguishes genuinely different facts within one concept/party, not `section_reference`.

Why not merge by value alone across ALL claim types: `TERMINATION_FEE_AMOUNT` is deliberately excluded from grouping. Citation-following never dispatches a follow-up call for an amount candidate (`handleFeeAmountCandidate` has no citation-followup branch), so no NEW amount duplicate can arise from this feature; grouping amounts by value would risk nothing this task's own evidence exercises, while adding a real risk of conflating the two $15,000,000.00 rows if some future, unrelated code path ever produced a coincidental match. Scoping the fix precisely to the two claim types the feature actually touches is the more conservative choice.

Precedence rule (`compareFeeSightingRank`), three criteria applied in order, each a tiebreak for the one before it:

1. **Direct corroboration beats borrowed corroboration, unconditionally.** A citation-followup claim's own `raw_value` is, by construction (`bare-citation-trigger-parser.js`), never operative ground text -- it is a cross-reference. Whenever its borrow succeeded, the section it borrowed FROM is -- by `resolveCitationFollowupTriggerCode`'s own gate, `!is_bare_citation` on every candidate it accepts -- always present this run as its own, independently (non-bare) corroborated candidate. A citation-followup claim therefore never has a stronger claim to being "the" sighting than the one it borrowed from, and this holds unconditionally, not just usually.
2. **Between two direct sightings, the more specific section reference wins** (`sectionReferenceDepth`: counts bracketed sub-clause groups -- "7.1" is 0, "7.1(c)(i)" is 2). This is the brief's own instruction made mechanical: "the cited sub-clause is often the BETTER evidence, since it carries the operative language." A deeper reference names the more precise clause a lawyer would actually cite, rather than the broader section that merely contains it.
3. **Between two equally-specific direct sightings, the longer evidence span wins** (`longestEvidenceSpan`) -- more operative text for a reviewer to read.
4. Final, absolute tiebreak: array processing order (`index`), so the outcome is a pure function of this run's own resolved set, never Map/sort iteration non-determinism.

Argued against the alternatives explicitly, per the brief's instruction: a rule that always preferred the ORIGINAL (pass-A) dispatch over a follow-up would be wrong (rejected explicitly by the brief, and would have picked Section 7.1's own whole-section reading over 7.1(c)(i)'s more precise one). A rule that always preferred the NARROWEST possible reference regardless of directness would wrongly let some hypothetical narrow citation-followup claim (which asserts nothing on its own) outrank a broader but genuinely-corroborating direct sighting. The chosen order -- directness first, specificity second -- is the only one of the three that is never wrong about WHICH sighting actually supports the fact, and only THEN reaches for which one states it more precisely.

**What survives, and what is recorded.** The winning claim keeps its own `subject_occurrence_id`, `claim_occurrence_id` and `evidence_ids` completely unchanged (nothing about it is re-derived) and gains one additive attribute, `duplicate_sightings_merged`, naming each folded sighting's `section_reference`, `claim_occurrence_id` and `raw_value`. This changes `claim_revision_id`/`closure_id` (attributes are part of that hash) but nothing else -- verified directly (see section 5). Every losing sighting is removed from `resolved` and, if a mirror is present, from `review_queue`; the winner's own `review_queue` mirror (if present) is repointed at its new revision/closure id, and one `DUPLICATE_FACT_SIGHTING_MERGED` residual is pushed per removed sighting, naming both sides. Nothing is silently dropped: a reader can see, on the winning row itself, that it represents several sightings and which one supplied its evidence, and can independently corroborate that from `resolution.residuals`.

## 4. Design for defect 2: deferred, real-lookup-only minting

Fixing the ordinal guess alone (look up `provisionsByGroupKey` instead of re-minting) is not sufficient in isolation, for two reasons discovered while designing the fix for defect 1:

First, an ordering problem independent of reconciliation: at the moment the citing candidate resolves, the cited candidate (typically a pass-B dispatch) has usually not been processed yet, so even a "check first" version of the old code would find nothing in `provisionsByGroupKey` and would have to fail closed most of the time anyway -- inconsistent, order-dependent behaviour.

Second, and decisively: once defect 1 is fixed, the citing claim is -- STRUCTURALLY, not coincidentally -- never the survivor whenever its own cited claim is present. `resolveCitationFollowupTriggerCode`'s gate guarantees the cited claim is always non-bare (direct), and the ranking rule above always ranks direct above borrowed. So whenever a `TRIGGERED_BY` relationship would have had a real target to point at, its own citing claim has, by that same run's own reconciliation, already been folded into that target -- there is no citing row left to be the relationship's source. Verified this is not merely a theoretical claim: replaying the real Modiv evidence, and independently re-deriving all three of this file's own pre-existing citation-following tests (`tests/canonical-v2-termination-fee-resolution.test.js`, originally lines ~911, ~964, ~1033 -- see section 6), shows the SAME pattern every time the cited section also independently corroborates (which the fee-side-family fallback makes the common case): the citing sighting is always the one that gets folded away.

Given this, the correct fix is not "guess more carefully" but "never guess" -- mint a `TRIGGERED_BY` relationship only from data already known to be published. Implementation:

- `handleFeeTriggerCandidate`'s citation-followup branch no longer calls `mintCitationTriggeredByRelationship` inline. It pushes a `pendingCitationRelationships` entry (`citing_section_reference`, `cited_references`, `fee_side`) -- everything needed to look BOTH sides up later, nothing speculative.
- A new function, `mintPendingCitationRelationships`, runs once, immediately after `reconcileDuplicateTerminationFeeSightings`, at the same point near the end of `resolveCandidates`. It builds a `section_reference -> resolvedEntry` map from the FINAL, post-reconciliation `resolved` array (`TERMINATION_FEE_TRIGGER` entries only), and for each pending relationship, looks up BOTH the citing section and each cited reference in that map. A relationship is minted only when both are found, using their REAL `provision_instance`/`claim` directly -- `mintCitationTriggeredByRelationship` itself no longer mints a provision at all; it now REQUIRES `citedProvision` (and `citingProvision`) as caller-supplied, already-real data. Missing on either side -> a `CITATION_RELATIONSHIP_MINT_FAILED` residual (reason `CITING_CLAIM_NOT_PUBLISHED` or `CITED_CLAIM_NOT_PUBLISHED`), never a guess.

This makes a dangling target impossible **by construction**, per the brief's own instruction: there is no code path left in `mintCitationTriggeredByRelationship` that invents an occurrence id nothing else in the write set also carries, because the function no longer invents occurrence ids at all.

**Honest consequence, stated plainly.** On the real Modiv evidence, and on every existing test scenario where a citation-followup borrow succeeds, this makes `mintPendingCitationRelationships` mint nothing (zero relationships, `resolution.relationships` omitted entirely) -- not because the mechanism is broken, but because reconciliation has already made the relationship's own reason for existing moot: the fact it would have connected is now one row, not two. The mechanism remains correct and is not dead code in general -- it still fires whenever a citing and cited claim resolve under genuinely DIFFERENT `concept_key`/`party` (so they never share a fact key and neither is folded into the other), a real if narrower case this design remains sound for. But it is honest to report that, for the shape of citation-following actually observed in the real evidence and in this feature's own existing test suite, defect 1's fix leaves defect 2's mechanism with nothing left to do.

## 11. What was proved, and what was judged

Kept deliberately separate, per the brief.

**Proved, by direct reads, code tracing, or an executable check, not by argument:**

- Both defects are real, in the committed evidence, exactly as the brief describes (section 1).
- Both defects' mechanism, read from the actual code, not inferred from symptoms (section 2): `provision_instance_id`'s hash payload includes the dispatched section's own span and a processing-order-dependent `ordinal`; `mintCitationTriggeredByRelationship` minted a fresh, hardcoded-`ordinal:1` guess instead of looking anything up.
- The precondition behind defect 2's fix (section 4): every one of this file's own pre-existing citation-following tests, and the real Modiv evidence, independently confirm the cited section always resolves as its own direct candidate whenever a borrow succeeds -- checked empirically per scenario, not assumed as a general law.
- The fix's effect on the real evidence: 9 resolved claims before, 6 after, the exact three rows that merged, the exact two winners, zero relationships, zero quarantines, full write-set validation clean (section 5).
- Occurrence-identity stability for every untouched and every winning claim (section 5, two independent methods: content-identity match against the committed run, and hermetic exact-id match via `git stash` and via a dedicated unit test with fully-known inputs).
- Inertness on the real, pre-existing three-call evidence: byte-identical `resolution_receipt_id` before and after this fix (section 9).
- The one full-suite failure is pre-existing and unrelated to this diff (section 10, `git stash` comparison).

**Judged, and argued for against the alternatives the brief itself raised:**

- That `(concept_key, party, claim_definition_key, canonical_value)` is the right identity for "is this the same fact" within this family, not a narrower per-clause identity and not a system-wide change to `provision_instance_id` (section 3). Grounded in how this codebase's own governed vocabulary already treats these seven trigger codes as closed legal categories, and in the fact that a narrower, purely evidence-span-based identity provably would not have converged Modiv's own three real sightings (checked directly, section 3).
- The three-criterion precedence order (directness, then specificity, then evidence length) as the rule for picking which sighting survives (section 3). The first criterion is proved safe unconditionally (resolveCitationFollowupTriggerCode's own gate guarantees it); the second and third are a judgement call about what a lawyer would rather read, argued from the brief's own wording and checked against every real case this evidence and this test suite provide, not against a hypothetical exhaustive set.
