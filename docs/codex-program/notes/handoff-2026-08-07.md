## What changed, and why each mattered

### The write half (`0993715`, `636dd11`)

`lib/canonical-v2/evidence-to-write-set-bridge.js` carries a run directory
into `canonical-writer.js`. The piece that was missing is
`lib/canonical-v2/admitted-source-chain-rebuild.js`: the writer does not
accept a pre-built admitted-source context, it takes four primitives, rebuilds
the context, and refuses unless the result is byte-identical to the
write-set's own `source_references`. That refusal is the point.

Four defects surfaced in the building, all fixed:

- **The runner never recorded its retrieval timestamp.** It passed
  `new Date().toISOString()` while reusing bytes fetched days earlier, and
  wrote it nowhere. That value feeds the whole identity chain, so no run could
  rebuild itself. Now pinned per deal to the pinning fetch's real timestamp.
- **`IMMUTABLE_SOURCE_DOCUMENT/V2` embeds a DEFLATE-output digest**, so the
  same document yields different identities on different machines. The fix is
  *not* a schema change — see DECISIONS.md for why that was rejected — but
  persisting the compressed source map and adopting it only after proving it
  inflates to the independently derived map.
- **The bridge imported a tenth of each run and succeeded**: 10 excerpts
  written, all 13 claims silently dropped, `accepted: true`. `adapter-result.json`
  carries no provisions; the runner adds them from `resolution.json`, and a
  claim with no governing provision is *dropped*, not rejected.
- **The writer resolves source references twice**, the second time against the
  transaction handle, which does not inherit the bridge's decoration. Every
  dry run passed and every real write failed.

### The baseline (`5828453`, `549ac8e`)

`scripts/canonical-v2-baseline-manifest.mjs` records, per run, what an import
*would* publish — not what the run claimed. Those differ, and the difference
is the whole point of having it. `npm run gate:baseline` checks it; it runs in
CI beside the invariants.

Three families that crashed mid-run on 2026-08-06 were recovered by replaying
the responses they had already recorded. A fourth, `CLOSING_CONDITIONS`, could
only be half-recovered: its section 6.2 "recorded response" is not a model
response at all but a captured CLI status message beginning
`Written to evidence/...`. **6.2 needs a live call.**

### The MAE pin review (`549ac8e`, corrected in `45f7b15`)

Step 2A's last open item. The generator's proposal was right: Modiv §8.12
contains both MAE definition sites. Pinned mechanically.

While reviewing it, `KEY_DEFINED_TERMS` turned out to be **aimed at the wrong
section** — §8.5 is *"Interpretation; Certain Definitions"*, the construction
conventions, while §8.12 is *"Definitions"*. The run correctly proposed
`"include"`, `"hereof"`, `"days"`, `"or"` and eleven more, every one of which
fell out as ungoverned. Pin corrected; **not yet re-run**, because there are no
recorded responses for 8.12 under that family.

## What a new implementer will get wrong

Seven things that are true and easy to miss. The second one caught me.

1. **"The write half is done" means in memory.** Every proof is against
   `InMemoryCanonicalRepository`. No Postgres, anywhere, has received the
   general runner's output; the JS writer and the 8,686-line SQL
   reimplementation have never been shown to agree on this data. None of the
   three core documents said so before today.

2. **Check the plan before reporting a finding.** Two of the five decisions I
   raised were already planned steps: the specific-performance filter is Step
   3C, and the representation, covenant and tax vocabularies are Step 3G —
   which even predicts the symptom (*"This is why the family resolved zero and
   put all 12 of its findings in open world"*). Both were withdrawn. This is
   the failure mode CLAUDE.md names, in its other direction: not "declaring
   built work missing" but "promoting settled work to a discovery", and it
   stops the work just as effectively. `grep -n "<the thing>" docs/core/PLAN.md`
   costs seconds.

3. **`adapter-result.json`'s write-set is not the one the run validated.** It
   carries no provisions. Import it directly and you publish the excerpts,
   lose every claim, and get `accepted: true`. The bridge composes the right
   thing; anything new that reads a run directly must too.

4. **`document_hash` is the deal identity, not `deal_key`.** `deal_key` is
   per-(deal, family) — the 24 committed Modiv runs carry 24 distinct ones
   over one document. **Code that groups by `deal_key` renders one family and
   looks correct.**

5. **`serving-client.js`'s RPCs look like the read path and are not.** Every
   `canonical_v2_active_*` RPC reads the Step 5A corpus-release layer, gated
   on a pointer table with zero rows — downstream of, and separate from, what
   the writer populates. Building the read half there produces a module that
   reads nothing and blames the pointer.

6. **Runs made before 2026-08-07 cannot be imported, and must not be made
   to.** They record neither their retrieval timestamp nor their compressed
   source map. Regenerate by replay — zero model calls. Do **not** re-derive
   `source_references` from a rebuild to make a run agree with itself; that is
   editing the evidence until it passes, and it does not even work, because
   `source_occurrence_id` is embedded in every excerpt row too.

7. **A family finding nothing and a family aimed at the wrong section produce
   the same observable.** The ladder's gate compares counts and cannot tell
   them apart. `KEY_DEFINED_TERMS` was the worked example. Every zero needs a
   reason recorded beside it — that is what Step 2B's triage table is for, and
   new zeros belong in it.

## Where to start on Monday

Nothing below needs a decision from anyone.

**1. Step 4A, first, and out of order.** It is written as a Stage 4 step and
it should run before Step 2C. "The write half is done" means *in memory*: the
bridge is proven against `InMemoryCanonicalRepository` only, and
`canonical_v2_write` — the 8,686-line SQL reimplementation of the same
contract — has never received the general runner's output. The two writers
have not been shown to agree on this data, and every step after 2B assumes
they do. 4A needs a local Postgres container and `psql`; no credential, no
production access, no decision.

Do it as table owner in that container and you also get the read half for
free — there is no `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` in the schema, so the grants
question (decision 1) does not arise locally. That converts a blocked decision
into a design review with a working prototype attached.

**2. Then Step 3G.** Four resolver defects, each located to a line, each an
ordinary bug rather than a taxonomy judgement, and all four corroborated
against the regenerated baseline last night with their real counts written
into the step — including which families they will and will *not* clear.
Largest single unblock left.

**3. Then Step 3C**, whose "establish the drop happens here before changing
anything" precondition is now discharged, and whose original quote-scope
hypothesis is refuted in the step by measurement. Ship it with the hostile
test the step requires; it is a loosening, and a loosening without one is how
a false positive gets shipped.

Then 3A, 3B, 3D–3F, same shape. Step 3H is genuinely decisions, not code.

**What is not worth starting:** anything in Stage 5 or 8 that assumes rows
are readable from a *hosted* database, and any live extraction run — that
costs money and should be a deliberate choice, not a warm-up. Two families
now have corrected pins that need a live call to mean anything
(`KEY_DEFINED_TERMS` on §8.12, `CONSIDERATION` on §2.6); they are cheap, but
they are still a spend.
