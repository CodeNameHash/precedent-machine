Ruling 1 · blocks the sitting

Sit with the packet as built, or fix it first

The 80-card anchor exists and is correctly designed — 8 seeded wrong claims, 4 planted party swaps and 4 planted wrong materiality codes. I checked the built packet against what the gate needs. Two defects, both cheap, both fatal to a one-shot sitting.

DefectWhat was builtWhat the gate needs
Seed count4 per hard class≈12 per hard classunderpowered
Card contextbare excerpt, e.g. 24 bytesgoverning sentence + chapeauunjudgeable

Why 4 seeds is not enough. The anchor's job is to score whether an adjudicator detects planted party swaps and wrong materiality codes — the two classes the publication gate treats as zero-tolerance. With 4 trials, an adjudicator catching 3 scores 75% with a confidence interval running roughly 30–95%. That cannot separate a good adjudicator from a poor one, which is the only thing the anchor exists to do.

Why the bare excerpt is not enough. Each card carries the excerpt and nothing around it. Card 6 reads "in all material respects" against a proposed MAT_MAE_QUALIFIED — judgeable, because the excerpt is the question. But 48 of the 80 cards are PARTY_ATTRIBUTION, SPAN or TOPIC_BUCKET, and you cannot tell whether the obligor is right without the sentence that names the obligor. As built, you would judge 16 cards properly and guess at 64 — and a partial ledger is rejected, so there is no way to submit only the 16.

Ruling 2 · unblocks the most work

Split the gate: resolving is not publishing

This is the error that produced a full build and no result, and it was mine.

There are two different acts and my plan collapsed them. Loosening a check so a claim resolves into the review queue puts it in front of a human. Publishing a claim sends it to a lawyer with nobody in between. The precision risk that justifies the whole calibration apparatus lives only in the second — but I gated both, so every measurable recovery was withheld to guard against a risk that does not apply to the queue.

That is why 2Y-F's 57 safe decisions, 2Y-I's 104 safe dispatches, 2Y-G's 59 duplicate suppressions and 2Y-H's 324 classifications are all sitting behind a human sitting that has not happened. None of them would put an unchecked claim in front of anyone.

Can resolver-side loosenings land live into the review queue, with publication still gated?

Rulings 5–7 · carried over, each has a default

Three that have been open since the plan was written

Open-world promotion threshold

21 concepts recur in three or more deals (146 occurrences), and 15 promotion cards are held awaiting this. At seven deals a percentage rule makes a single recurrence 14%, which is why concept-recurrence reads better than a percentage. Promoting badly writes noise into the taxonomy permanently, and taxonomy mistakes corrupt precedent search — the product.

Does each family carry a stated recall floor?

A precision-only rule is a ratchet: it can freeze the system at excellent precision and unusable coverage, which fails your standard as surely as publishing wrongly. The counterweight would be a per-family floor derived from category-summary-features.js. The catch: that file is a V1 artefact of expected shapes, not per-deal truth, and a flat floor collides with the rule that a family returning zero can be correct — guaranty on an unfinanced deal.

What false-publication rate is acceptable for a claim no human sees?

This gates 2Y-0A, the only step that changes what a user sees without a human in the loop. The calibration harness can measure the rate at any looseness rung; it cannot tell you which one you are willing to ship. There is no sensible default here — it is a product judgement.
